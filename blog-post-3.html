The 10-minute habit that makes movement easier.

The hardest part of exercise is often not the exercise. It is crossing the invisible line between thinking about it and starting.

THE 10-MINUTE RULEMake the start
small enough
to say yes.

When a workout feels like it needs an hour, special clothes, and a perfect schedule, it is easy to postpone. A ten-minute commitment changes the question from “Can I complete a full workout?” to “Can I begin?”

Lower the starting line

Walk around the block. Stretch while your kettle boils. Do two rounds of squats, wall push-ups, and a plank. You are allowed to stop at ten minutes. Often, starting creates enough momentum to continue — but continuing is a bonus, not the requirement.

The real win is teaching your brain that movement belongs in your day. Repetition makes the habit feel less like a negotiation and more like brushing your teeth.

Attach it to something you already do

Choose a reliable cue: after your morning coffee, before your shower, or just after closing your laptop. Put your shoes where you can see them. Remove one small piece of friction before tomorrow arrives.

Fitness grows from showing up. Let ten minutes be enough to begin building that trust with yourself.
