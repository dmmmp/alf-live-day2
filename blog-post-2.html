How to build a plate that keeps you full.

A satisfying meal is not about eating less and hoping for the best. It is about giving your body the building blocks it needs.

THE SIMPLE PLATE½ plants
¼ protein
¼ energy

When people feel hungry soon after a meal, the problem is often not willpower. The meal may simply be missing volume, protein, fiber, or enough energy to carry them through the next few hours.

Think in three parts

Start by filling half your plate with plants: vegetables, salad, fruit, or beans. Add a quarter plate of protein such as eggs, fish, chicken, tofu, Greek yogurt, or lentils. Finish with a quarter plate of energy-giving carbohydrates like rice, potatoes, oats, or bread.

Then add a little fat for flavor and staying power — olive oil, avocado, nuts, or a dressing you genuinely enjoy. This is a framework, not a test. The portions can change depending on your hunger and activity.

Make it easy to repeat

Keep two proteins and two quick vegetables in your kitchen. Batch-cook one carbohydrate. Give yourself a few reliable combinations for the days when decision-making is the last thing you want to do.

A balanced plate is not about earning food or avoiding pleasure. It is a practical way to feel nourished enough to make your next choice with a clear head.
